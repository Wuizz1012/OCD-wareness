The OCD Cycle: Obsessions & Compulsions

Understand the two core components that drive Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

Component 1

Obsessions (Intrusive Thoughts)

Unwanted, distressing, and repetitive thoughts, urges, or mental images that enter a person's mind involuntarily, triggering overwhelming anxiety and fear.

  • Intense fear of contamination, germs, or illnesses.
  • Distressing fear that harm will come to oneself or loved ones.
  • Need for exactness, symmetry, or perfect alignment.
Component 2

Compulsions (Repetitive Behaviors)

Repetitive physical behaviors or mental rituals that a person feels driven to perform in order to temporarily relieve or prevent the distress caused by obsessions.

  • Repetitive handwashing or cleaning according to strict rules.
  • Repeatedly checking doors, locks, or appliances.
  • Arranging objects in an exact, rigid pattern.

Common Subtypes of OCD

Checking

Persistent fear of potential danger or harm, leading to constant re-checking of locks, switches, or tasks.

Contamination

Overwhelming distress around germs, dirt, or illness, resulting in ritualized cleaning or washing.

Symmetry & Order

Urgent need to have items arranged in a specific, balanced, or "just right" manner.

Intrusive Thoughts

Sudden, disturbing mental images or ideas that run completely counter to the person's values.

Empathy Starts with True Understanding

If you or a loved one are struggling with obsessive thoughts or compulsive behaviors, remember that you are not alone. OCD is manageable and treatable with professional care and supportive environments.

Seek Professional Care

Reach out to licensed psychologists or mental health experts specializing in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT/ERP).

Listen Without Judgment

Avoid dismissing or teasing individuals about their compulsions. Offer patience and create a safe emotional space.

Spread Awareness

Share accurate information to help eliminate the casual slang usage of "OCD" in everyday conversations.
